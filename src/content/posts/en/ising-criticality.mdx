Near a continuous phase transition, the correlation length diverges as

$$
 \xi \sim |T - T_c|^{-\nu}
$$

The system thus loses a natural finite length scale. Similar fluctuation patterns appear across scales, from the lattice spacing to macroscopic distances.

## Correlation Functions

Away from the critical point, the connected correlation function typically decays exponentially:

$$
 G(r) \sim \frac{\exp(-r / \xi)}{r^{d - 2 + \eta}} .
$$

At $ T = T_c $, $ \xi \to \infty $: the exponential cutoff disappears, leaving a power law. This scale-free behavior makes the renormalization group a natural language for describing critical phenomena.

## Where Universality Comes From

Coarse graining forgets many microscopic details while retaining a few structural features, such as dimensionality, symmetry, and the range of interactions. Different materials can therefore flow to the same fixed point and share critical exponents.
